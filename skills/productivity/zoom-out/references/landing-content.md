# Landing method for content and account work

Applies to: the topic, framework, hook, title and cover, and bolded key points of a single video or image-and-text post, plus account positioning, account stage and agenda setting. Once zoom out has judged where the problem sits, use this file to check the work and land it.

## 1. Parent chain

Looking up from a single piece of content, the levels in a content task are:

Single piece → Content sequence → Account stage → Account positioning and target audience → Business goal and product

| Level | Facts needed | What it changes about this piece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single piece | Material, topic (the reason to watch), theme (the mechanism or judgement to make clear) | Framework, hook, ending |
| Content sequence | Related published content, which piece this one follows on from, what it sets up for the next one | Whether it repeats, how far to take it, what to leave for the next one |
| Account stage | Whether the account is at an early, middle or late stage, whether it is transitioning, the current stage goal | Whether this piece should prioritise attracting new users, building trust, or carrying the product |
| Account positioning and target audience | Target audience, content lines, value boundary | The reader entry point and case selection |
| Business goal and product | Current product, validation progress | The closing action, whether to mention the product |

Check level by level, and keep only the levels that would change the judgement for this piece. When a level would not change the framework, the hook, the ending or the decision to publish, stop at the level below it.

## 2. Agenda check: judge the account stage and this piece's role

The agenda is what the account keeps talking about, and from what angle, so that users form an impression of it. Check three questions:

1. **Long-term agenda**: when users think of this account, which two or three judgements or questions should they associate with it?
2. **Stage agenda**: which stage is the account at now (starting out, validating, scaling, transitioning), and which one is it mainly repeating at this stage?
3. **This piece's role**: does this piece strengthen the current agenda, open a new agenda, or drift off the agenda? Is it mainly responsible for attracting new users, deepening the impression existing users have, or carrying the product?

The first two questions decide where this piece belongs; the third decides its framework, hook and ending.

## 3. Where the facts come from, and what to do when they are missing

1. First scan the user's positioning profile, content collection and work in progress, and answer the three questions above.
2. For the parts you cannot find, infer from existing content: for example, infer the long-term agenda from the themes of published content, and infer the stage from the number of posts, the product and the validation progress. Mark anything inferred as "inferred from ...".
3. When the point still cannot be judged and it would change the conclusion, give the judgement and the fix for the most likely case first, and ask only one question at the end of the reply. Ask the third question by default, with options, for example:

> What is this video mainly meant to achieve this time? A: attract new users; B: deepen existing users' impression of one of your judgements; C: carry the product; D: try a new direction.

When the user's answer would change the framework, give the matching fix for that answer. Do not work up through the single piece, the content sequence and the account stage one by one: those are the internal checking order, not questions to put to the user.

## 4. What a compelling case does inside an account

Material, topic and theme are three different things: material is the original experience or case; the topic is the reason the target audience is willing to spend time watching; the theme is the mechanism or judgement this piece eventually has to make clear.

Check in order:

1. For the target audience, what is this case's strongest reason to watch?
2. Which judgement tied to the account positioning can it make clear?
3. Has existing content in the account already covered this judgement? If it has, what does this piece add?
4. At the account's current stage, is it better suited to attracting new users, building trust, or carrying the product?

When a case is compelling but has no natural connection to the account positioning, say so directly. Whether to publish it, and on which platform, is the user's decision; do not force jargon in just to look closer to the positioning.

## 5. Two common biases

- **Using the account positioning in place of a content centre**: the account positioning decides which entry point the reader comes in through; the content centre has to be found from the whole text, by choosing the judgement that runs through every scene in it.
- **Only swapping words locally**: when the title, hook, signature and bolded points keep getting reworded, check the division of labour across the whole piece first: what each part is responsible for giving the reader. When the division is wrong, swapping words cannot fix it.

## 6. Landing spec

When handing the work back to the original task, write down:

- **Where**: which part changes (topic, framework, hook, title and cover, a paragraph of the body, the ending, the bolded points);
- **Function**: what the reader should get from that part once it is changed;
- **Must not break**: the finalised title, judgements the user has confirmed, platform rules;
- **Acceptance**: for example, from the title, the cover and the first five seconds alone, can the target audience say what this piece is about and why it concerns them.
