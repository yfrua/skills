# Landing method for text edits

Applies to: edits to sentences, paragraphs, references and wording. Once zooming out has identified where the problem sits, use this file to decide how to change it.

## Levels

The levels for a text task are: word → sentence → paragraph (how neighbouring sentences join) → the argument structure of the whole piece → the writing intent and the reader. When checking, work upward from the word or sentence that shows the problem, and stop at the level that explains it.

## How to fix an unclear reference

Zooming out can often establish that no word needs swapping and that an antecedent sentence belongs in front of the reference, but the placement and the wording of that sentence are where this most easily goes wrong.

Set the scope first: sentences the user has already finalised, or has explicitly said to keep, stay untouched; the original word (such as "this") is kept by default, and the first aim is to make it clear through context.

Then compare three fixes and choose the smallest change that solves the problem:

1. **Swap the word**: replace "this" with a noun phrase. Fits when the referent sits in the sentence just before and there are only two candidates.
2. **Add an antecedent sentence**: add a sentence before "this" that names the referent. Fits when the object appears scattered across the earlier text and the reader needs it gathered up first.
3. **Adjust the structure**: reorder sentences, merge two sentences, or split one. Fits when the unclear reference is caused by the sentence order.

When you choose to add an antecedent sentence, compare positions first: list every gap between sentences from where the referent last appears to the sentence holding "this", imagine the sentence in each one in turn, pick the spot that reads most naturally and needs the fewest changes to what follows, and say why the other positions were not chosen. After insertion, the sentence must not come between the two halves of a transition or contrast pair the original already has (for example "although ... but ..." or "on the one hand ... on the other hand ...").

The sentence you add must satisfy all of these at once:

- **Name it**: use a noun phrase to say plainly what "this" is meant to point at, so that it becomes the nearest and most visible candidate to "this";
- **Connect to the text before**: keep the same logical relation as the previous sentence (continuation, summary, contrast or cause and effect), not a standalone summary sentence;
- **Add nothing**: introduce no new claim and no new information, and do not repeat what the earlier text already said;
- **Stay close to the original**: keep length and tone in line with the neighbouring sentences, do not break the contrasts and rhythm the original already has, and keep it to one sentence that is usually no longer than its neighbours;
- **Serve what follows**: once it is in, the sentence holding "this" stands as written with no further change.

Acceptance: read the added sentence plus the sentence holding "this" on their own first, then put them back into the paragraph and read again; ask "what does this point at" and there must be only one answer, and exactly the one the author meant. If the sentence holding "this" also has to be changed before it reads smoothly, the position or the fix was the wrong choice; go back and compare again.
